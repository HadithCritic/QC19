Is N divisible by P?
-------------------------------------------------------------
Algorithm for finding if a number N is divisible by a prime P
©2003-2004 Stu Savory
https://savory.de/maths1.htm
-------------------------------------------------------------
# Numbers have two parts, front and rear (fr), N = 10f + r.
1. Find the smallest multiple m of P to produce a number (fr) with r=1 or r=9:
	fr = m * P
	If r=1, then	x = -f
	If r=9, then	x = f+1

# N = Front and Rear parts (FR):
2. 	Let N = FR
	Loop
	    n = F + R*x
	Until n is divisible by P or not.
  If n is divisible by P, then N is divisible by P too, else not.


Example:
Is 403 divisibile by 13?
The smallest multiplier (m) to make 13*m ends in 1 or 9 is m = 3 so 13*m = 39.
39 ends in 9 thus x = f+1 = 3+1 = 4.
403
40 + 4*3 = 52
           5 + 4*2 = 13 ==> Yes.

------------------
Mathematical Proof
------------------
Does P divide N?
Let           N = 10f + r
Let m = smallest multiplier of P to make mP ends in 1 or 9
If  mP ends in 1, then x =   - f
If  mP ends in 9, then x = 1 + f
Does P divide f + xr? If yes, then P divides N.

N = 10*front + rear :            N   =       10f  +  r
multiply by x       :           xN   =       10xf + xr
add      mP*f       :     mPf + xN   = mPf + 10xf + xr
if P divides N = Pn :     mPf + xPn  =   f        + xr
                        P(m f + x n) =   f        + xr
then                :   P   divides      f        + xr

Proof Example for P = 17:
Three times 17 is 51 which is the smallest multiple of 17 that ends in a 1 or 9.
Since it's a 1 so we are going to SUBTRACT five times the remainder r.
N = 10f + r
Multiply both sides by x = -5:         -5N       = -50f - 5r
Add 51f to both sides:            51f - 5N       =    f - 5r
If N is divisible by P:         3*17f - 5(17*n)  =    f - 5r
                                17(3f - 5    n)  =    f - 5r
then                            17 divides            f - 5r



------------------------
Unit, Primes, Composites
------------------------

1 is the Unit. It is the father of all numbers.

Primes are the mothers of their multiples.
  Ex: 2 is the mother of the   even numbers.
  Ex: 3 is the mother of the 3-even numbers.
  or  p is the mother of the p-even numbers.

Composites are the children.
  Ex: 6  has two different mothers, 2 and 3.
  Ex: 9  has two same      mothers, 3 and 3.

In chemistry:
	1          == proton
	Prime      == atom
	Composite  == molecule

In biology:
	1          == DNA base
	Prime      == gene
	Composites == chromosome


DNA is made of chemical building blocks called nucleotides:
Phosphate, Sugar, and Nitrogen base (Adenine, Thymine, Guanine, or Cytosine).
To form a strand of DNA, nucleotides are linked into chains,
with alternating phosphate and sugar groups.

The sequence of bases determines the biological instructions of a DNA strand.

For example, the sequence ATCGTT might instruct for blue eyes,
       while the sequence ATCGCT might instruct for brown eyes.
The complete DNA instruction book (genome) for a human contains about
3 billion bases and about 20000 genes on 23 pairs of chromosomes.
https://genome.gov/25520880/
